Before your first workout

Start with Workout A. If your first session is Wednesday, do A on Wednesday, B on Friday and A on Sunday. Then continue alternating B / A / B with at least one day between lifting sessions.

Warm-up before every weights session: 10–15 minutes

Do this before Workout A and B in Phase 1, and before every Upper A, Lower A, Upper B and Lower B session in Phases 2–3. The warm-up prepares you for the movements you are about to do; it should not tire you out.

Step 1 — Raise your body temperature (5 minutes)
Choose treadmill walking, stationary bike or elliptical. Start easy for 2 minutes, then gradually increase to a brisk but comfortable pace for 3 minutes. You should feel warmer and breathe a little faster, but still be able to talk.
Step 2 — Dynamic movement preparation (3–4 minutes)
  • Arm circles: 10 forward + 10 backward.
  • Shoulder-blade squeezes: 10 slow repetitions.
  • Bodyweight squats: 10 controlled repetitions.
  • Hip hinges: 10 repetitions; push hips back with a neutral spine.
  • Easy alternating reverse lunges: 5 each leg, holding a support if needed. Skip if uncomfortable.
Step 3 — Specific warm-up sets (2–6 minutes)
Before the first demanding lift, do 1–3 progressively heavier warm-up sets. These are not working sets and should feel easy.

Which warm-up sets should I do?

WorkoutBefore working sets
Phase 1 — Workout AChest press: 1–2 light sets (12 reps, then 6–8 reps). Leg press: 1 light set of 10 reps.
Phase 1 — Workout BIncline chest press: 1–2 light sets. Goblet squat: 1 easy bodyweight squat set, then 1 very light dumbbell set. RDL: practise 8 unloaded hip hinges first.
Phase 2/3 — Upper ABench press: 2–3 ramp-up sets (e.g. empty/light bar × 10, then a moderate load × 5–6). Do not fatigue yourself.
Phase 2/3 — Lower AHack squat: 2 light ramp-up sets. Leg press: 1 light set if needed.
Phase 2/3 — Upper BIncline dumbbell press: 1–2 light sets before working weight.
Phase 2/3 — Lower BRDL: 2–3 very light practice/ramp-up sets; begin with an unloaded hip hinge if technique needs practice. Bulgarian split squat: 1 easy bodyweight set per leg.

For the remaining exercises: if the muscle/joint is already warm and the first set is light, you usually do not need separate warm-up sets for every isolation exercise. If an exercise feels stiff or unfamiliar, do one easy practice set.

Use lighter loads than your working weight. Warm-up reps should never be close to failure. Increase the number of ramp-up sets if the working weight is heavy; reduce them if the load is light.

Warm-up on cardio-only days

Start with 3–5 minutes at an easy pace, then gradually increase to your planned brisk walking, cycling or elliptical pace. Finish with 2–3 minutes easy. No strenuous stretching is required.

5 AM or 9 PM?

Both work. Muscle growth does not meaningfully depend on the time of day. Choose the time you can maintain while getting 7–9 hours of sleep.

  • 5 AM: prepare clothes and breakfast the night before. Drink water; a banana is optional if you prefer a little food first.
  • 9 PM: fine if it does not push bedtime later or disturb your sleep. If you feel wired afterwards, train earlier.
  • Non-negotiable: do not cut sleep to fit training in.

How hard should sets feel?

For the first weeks, finish most sets feeling you could still do about 2–3 good repetitions. No max attempts and no forced reps.

Workout A

1Chest press machine2 × 10–12
  • Adjust the seat so the handles are around mid-chest height.
  • Keep your back against the pad and feet flat.
  • Push forward until your arms are almost straight; do not forcefully lock the elbows.
  • Bring the handles back slowly and under control.
  • Keep elbows slightly below shoulder level; do not let the weights slam.
Targets: chest, triceps and front shoulders.
Record a working set
2Lat pulldown2 × 10–12
  • Secure thighs under the pads and grip the bar slightly wider than shoulder width.
  • Sit tall with only a slight backward lean.
  • Pull the bar towards your upper chest by driving your elbows down.
  • Let the bar rise slowly; keep control throughout.
  • Do not pull behind your neck or swing your torso.
Targets: lats and upper back, with biceps assisting.
Record a working set
3Leg press2 × 10–12
  • Place feet about shoulder width apart on the platform.
  • Release the safety handles and lower the platform until your knees are comfortably bent.
  • Push through the whole foot to return; do not lock your knees.
  • Do not descend so far that your lower back curls away from the pad.
Targets: quadriceps, glutes and some hamstrings.
Record a working set
4Seated cable row2 × 10–12
  • Sit upright with knees slightly bent and arms extended.
  • Pull the handle towards your lower ribs or abdomen.
  • Squeeze your shoulder blades together without shrugging.
  • Return slowly; avoid rounding your back or rocking your torso.
Targets: mid-back, lats and biceps.
Record a working set
5Seated leg curl2 × 12–15
  • Align your knee joint with the machine pivot.
  • Place the lower-leg pad just above your ankles.
  • Curl your heels down and back, pause briefly, then return slowly.
  • Keep your hips against the seat.
Targets: hamstrings.
Record a working set
6Lateral raise machine2 × 12–15
  • Sit with your arms against the machine pads.
  • Raise your arms sideways to approximately shoulder height.
  • Lower slowly. Avoid shrugging, swinging or using momentum.
Targets: side deltoids (shoulders).
Record a working set
7Forearm plank2 × 20–30 sec
  • Place elbows directly beneath shoulders.
  • Keep your body in a straight line; tighten abdomen and glutes.
  • Do not let your lower back sag or hips rise too high.
  • Stop when you can no longer maintain position.
Targets: core stability.
Record a working set

Workout B

1Incline chest press machine2 × 10–12
  • Set the seat so handles begin around upper-chest height.
  • Keep your back against the pad and feet planted.
  • Press forward smoothly, then lower under control.
  • Avoid excessively flaring your elbows or locking them forcefully.
Targets: upper chest, triceps and front shoulders.
Record a working set
2Chest-supported row2 × 10–12
  • Adjust the chest pad so you can hold it comfortably against your chest.
  • Pull the handles towards your torso and squeeze your shoulder blades.
  • Return slowly without lifting your chest off the pad.
Targets: upper/middle back, lats and biceps.
Record a working set
3Goblet squat2 × 10
  • Hold one dumbbell vertically against your chest.
  • Stand about shoulder width apart, toes turned slightly out if comfortable.
  • Bend hips and knees, lowering only as far as you can keep a stable torso and planted feet.
  • Push through your feet to stand; keep knees tracking in the same direction as toes.
Targets: quadriceps and glutes.
Record a working set
4Seated shoulder press machine2 × 10–12
  • Set handles around shoulder height and keep your back supported.
  • Press upwards without arching your lower back.
  • Lower until elbows are comfortably bent; do not force a deep stretch.
Targets: shoulders and triceps.
Record a working set
5Dumbbell Romanian deadlift2 × 10
  • Hold dumbbells in front of your thighs; keep a slight bend in your knees.
  • Push hips backwards while lowering dumbbells close to your legs.
  • Stop when you feel a hamstring stretch and can maintain a neutral back.
  • Drive hips forward to stand. This is a hip hinge, not a squat.
Targets: hamstrings and glutes. Start very light and ask a trainer to check your hip hinge.
Record a working set
6Dumbbell biceps curl2 × 10–12
  • Hold dumbbells with palms facing forward and elbows close to your sides.
  • Curl towards your shoulders without moving your upper arms.
  • Lower slowly; do not swing your torso.
Targets: biceps.
Record a working set
7Rope triceps pushdown2 × 10–12
  • Attach a rope to a high cable pulley and hold one end in each hand.
  • Keep elbows close to your torso.
  • Push down until arms are almost straight; separate rope ends slightly at the bottom.
  • Return slowly without letting elbows drift forwards.
Targets: triceps.
Record a working set

  • Tuesday: 20–30 minutes brisk treadmill walking; start around 2–4% incline if comfortable.
  • Thursday: 20–30 minutes bike or elliptical, followed by gentle mobility if desired.

Phase 3 — Continued muscle development

Months 5–6Continue upper/lower · 4 days/week

This is not a brand-new split. Keep the Phase 2 schedule and exercises. The aim is to progress the programme rather than change exercises just for novelty.

  • Main compound movements: gradually increase weight while maintaining good technique.
  • Chest and back: work towards 3 sets per exercise where recovery is good.
  • Legs: work towards 3 sets on the main movements; keep accessory volume manageable.
  • Shoulders and arms: generally 2–3 sets per exercise.
  • Core: increase plank duration or cable-crunch resistance gradually.
  • Cardio: 2 easy sessions of 20–30 minutes if recovery allows.

Keep a training log

Record exercise, machine setting, weight, reps and sets every session. This is how you verify progress rather than guessing.

Recovery and safety

  • Sleep 7–9 hours where possible.
  • Keep most sets 2–3 reps short of failure initially.
  • Sharp joint pain or pain that changes your movement is a reason to stop and reassess.
  • Ask a McFIT trainer to demonstrate unfamiliar equipment and check your squat, bench press and Romanian deadlift technique.
  • Do not try to complete every exercise on day one. Learn a few movements properly.

Body recomposition basics

  • Protein target: approximately 120–145 g/day.
  • Start around 2,000–2,100 kcal/day only as a provisional estimate; adjust after 2–3 weeks based on average weight and waist trend.
  • Track morning body weight several times per week and compare weekly averages.
  • Measure waist every two weeks and take progress photos monthly.
  • Do not expect belly fat to disappear from ab exercises; overall fat loss reduces abdominal fat.
